Field Investigations

4 Real-World Case Studies Deconstructed

Examining actual scam mechanisms reported across Indian cities and how attackers leveraged social engineering to compromise individuals and businesses.

High Severity Scam

Case 1: The 'Digital Arrest' & Fake Customs Threat

A retired educator received an IVR call claiming a parcel sent to Cambodia with passports and contraband had been seized in their name. Scammers transferred the victim to fake 'officers' on Skype with artificial police backdrops. The victim was kept on a 36-hour continuous video call and coerced into transferring ₹42 lakhs to a 'Supreme Court verification escrow'.

The Threat Hook: The Social Engineering Hook: Overwhelming fear, simulated judicial authority, and social isolation.
Defense Shield: The Defense Countermeasure: Remember: No Indian agency conducts 'Digital Arrest'. Immediately hang up and dial 1930.
Mass Smishing Attack

Case 2: The 9:30 PM Electricity Disconnection SMS

Tens of thousands of apartment residents received an SMS from an ordinary 10-digit mobile number stating: 'Dear Consumer, your electricity power will be disconnected at 9:30 PM tonight due to unpaid previous month bill. Call Electricity Officer immediately at 98XXXX.' Panicked residents called, were told to pay a nominal ₹10 test fee via a remote screen-share app (QuickSupport), which allowed scammers to read their banking OTPs and siphon funds.

The Threat Hook: The Social Engineering Hook: False emergency threatening daily comfort and tiny monetary entry barrier (₹10).
Defense Shield: The Defense Countermeasure: Official discoms never send disconnection notices via personal WhatsApp or 10-digit mobile numbers.
Peer-to-Peer Marketplace Fraud

Case 3: The 'Scan to Receive Payment' Olx Trick

A college student posted a study table for sale online for ₹3,000. A buyer claiming to be an army officer agreed without bargaining and sent a QR code over WhatsApp, instructing: 'Scan this QR code on Google Pay / PhonePe to instantly receive the advance money in your bank.' The student entered their 6-digit UPI PIN thinking it was a confirmation, only to find ₹15,000 debited from their savings account.

The Threat Hook: The Social Engineering Hook: Patriotic trust (impersonating defense personnel) and exploiting confusion over UPI PIN mechanics.
Defense Shield: The Defense Countermeasure: Universal Rule: Entering a UPI PIN ALWAYS deducts money from your account. Receiving money never needs a PIN.
Enterprise Data Breach

Case 4: Local Pathology Lab Customer Records Leak

A diagnostic center network maintained patient phone numbers, addresses, and test histories on an unauthenticated Google Drive cloud folder. Threat actors scraped 12,000 records and called patients impersonating doctors, quoting exact medical test dates and demanding urgent advance deposits for prescription drugs. Patients believed the calls because the caller knew confidential personal medical details.

The Threat Hook: The Social Engineering Hook: Leveraging leaked Personally Identifiable Information (PII) to build unshakeable credibility.
Defense Shield: The Defense Countermeasure: Data fiduciaries must encrypt customer records at rest and adhere to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP).
